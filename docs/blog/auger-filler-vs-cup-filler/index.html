Auger Filler vs Volumetric Cup Filler: Which Do You Need?

By the FOOD LINE engineering team · Updated 2026-11-17

Short answer: Use an auger (screw) filler for powders such as milk powder, flour, coffee, protein and spices, especially fine, dusty or poor-flowing ones. Use a volumetric cup filler for free-flowing, uniform granules such as granulated sugar, salt, rice and seeds: it is simpler and costs less, but it cannot dose powders that do not flow freely.

How do an auger filler and a cup filler work?

An auger filler uses a servo-driven screw under a hopper. Each turn of the screw pushes out a set volume of powder, so the fill is set by the number of turns. An agitator in the hopper keeps poor-flowing powder moving into the screw, and a closed hopper with a dust-suction port keeps fine powder away from the seal area.

A volumetric cup filler has a row of measuring cups on a rotating plate. Each cup is filled level from the hopper and then emptied into the bag, stick or sachet. The cup volume is sized to your target weight and product density. There is no screw to compress or grind the product, so the cups are fast, simple and gentle on crystals.

Which products need an auger filler?

Powders that are fine, dusty, sticky or do not flow freely need an auger:

  • Milk powder, milk-tea powder, creamer and 3-in-1 coffee
  • Flour, starch and protein powder
  • Seasoning, chilli, curry and other spice powders
  • Icing sugar, sweetener powder and sugar blended with cocoa or creamer
  • Detergent, putty and chemical powders (SUS316L contact parts available for corrosive products)

For very fine powder we add a vacuum feeder and an anti-drip auger. See our powder packing solutions and spice powder packing machines.

Which products suit a volumetric cup filler?

Cups work best for free-flowing granules of uniform size and steady bulk density:

  • Granulated white and brown sugar
  • Salt
  • Rice, beans and seeds in small packs
  • Other small, dry, free-flowing granules

Cups are not suitable for irregular solids such as chips, nuts or candy, which are weighed by a multihead weigher, or for powders that bridge or cake in the cup. For sugar sticks and sachets see our sugar stick packing machines.

Which is more accurate: auger or cup filler?

Both fillers measure by volume, so accuracy depends on how consistent the product's density is. A servo auger is typically within ±1 % for fills above 100 g and about ±1–2 g below 100 g, depending on powder density and flowability. A cup filler is accurate when the granule is uniform; if density changes from batch to batch, the weight changes with it, and the cup volume must be adjusted or a different set of cups fitted.

If you need tighter weight control on granules, use a linear weigher or a multihead weigher (±1 g up to 100 g, ±1 % above 100 g) instead of cups, and add a check weigher to reject under-weight packs.

Which costs more?

A volumetric cup filler is the lowest-cost dosing system: it has few moving parts and no servo screw. A servo auger with agitator costs more, and a multihead weigher costs more again. If your product flows freely, cups save money; if it is a powder, an auger is the right choice even though it costs more, because cups cannot dose it reliably. Read what sets packing machine price.

How to decide in one minute

  • Is it a powder, or does it cake, bridge or make dust? → Auger filler.
  • Is it a dry granule that pours like sugar or salt, with uniform size? → Volumetric cups.
  • Is it irregular or high-value (nuts, chips, candy)? → Multihead weigher.
  • Is it a liquid or paste? → Piston or pump filler.

FAQ

Can one machine use both an auger and cups?

The bag-forming mainframe is the same, so we can supply both dosing sets for one machine. Changing between them takes a planned changeover, so most buyers order the doser for their main product and a second set only if they run another product regularly.

Can a cup filler pack powder?

Only powders that flow very freely. Fine, dusty or sticky powders such as milk powder, flour, protein or icing sugar bridge in the cups and give uneven fills, so they need a servo auger with an agitator.

Is an auger filler good for granulated sugar?

It works, but cups are the better choice: they are faster, cost less and do not grind the crystals. Use an auger only when the sugar is powdered or blended with powders such as creamer or cocoa.
