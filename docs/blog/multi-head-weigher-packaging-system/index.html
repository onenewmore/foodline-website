Multi-Head Weigher Packaging System: How It Works and How to Choose One

By the FOOD LINE engineering team · Updated 2026-11-24

What is a multi-head weigher packaging system? A multi-head weigher packaging system is a computer-controlled weigh-and-bag line that spreads product across independently measured hoppers, selects the hopper combination closest to the target weight, and discharges that dose into a bagger or filler. Plants use it when pieces are irregular — snacks, nuts, granules, dried fruit, frozen foods, or small hardware — and giveaway must stay tight.

FOOD LINE supplies this as a complete weigh-and-bag cell: a Z-type elevator, a 10-head or 14-head combination weigher, a support platform, a VFFS bagger and an output conveyor, with optional check weighing and metal detection. The published multi-function vertical snack packer covers pillow, gusset, block-bottom, stand-up, hanging-hole and linked bags at 20–70 bags/min, depending on frame size and product. That cell is an automated multi-head weighing packaging line — the weigher assembles the dose, and the bagger must receive it in time.

How combination weigher packaging machinery actually reaches the target

The industry also calls this combination weigher packaging machinery. Unlike a linear scale, it never relies on a single portion.

Product arrives on a conveyor or bucket elevator and lands on a distribution cone. Vibratory feeders move a partial portion into a pool hopper, then into a weigh hopper. Each hopper is measured on its own load cell. The controller opens the combination closest to the target, preferably without going under the legal or commercial minimum. Those hoppers refill, and the cycle repeats.

As a general weighing principle, with n eligible weigh hoppers, the controller can consider up to 2ⁿ − 1 combinations. Ten heads yield 1,023 possible subsets; fourteen heads yield 16,383. More heads mean more ways to assemble a tight dose, not a more precise load cell, so a well-tuned 14-head machine can be both faster and closer to target on the same irregular product. That advantage disappears if hoppers are starved or slow to empty.

On FOOD LINE’s published guidance, a 10-head weigher suits most snacks up to about 60 weighments/min, while a 14-head weigher is better for higher-speed lines or expensive products such as nuts and dried fruit. Decide on accepted good bags per minute on your product and film, not on a catalogue maximum.

The weigher has to deliver the dose as one coherent slug. If the product tail is still falling when the sealing jaws close, a correctly weighed portion can be split or trapped in the seal. Timing hoppers and a ready-to-discharge handshake belong to the same system. Key components that control accuracy, hygiene, speed and seal quality

Head count is only one specification. The parts below decide whether granule weighing and packing equipment still holds tolerance after washdown and changeover.

Load cell sensor. Each weigh hopper depends on a load cell sensor that can resolve small differences and stay stable under vibration, temperature change and occasional overload. Auto-zero, frame isolation and washdown protection matter more in production than a headline resolution figure. A noisy or slow-settling cell feeds the controller bad data, and extra heads will not fix it.

Stainless steel weighing hopper. A stainless steel weighing hopper is the food-contact vessel that holds each partial portion. Volume must suit the target: good combinations often use three or four hoppers, so each should hold a fraction of the dose, with room for the largest pieces. Dimpled or Teflon-coated interiors are used when product is sticky, sugary or frozen. FOOD LINE lists 1.6 L and 2.5 L buckets on its 10-head and 14-head weighers, plus special versions for noodles and salad, on the check weigher machine and auxiliary equipment page.

Servo drive motor. On the bagger, a servo drive motor — and servo film transport — controls how evenly the film is pulled, registered and presented to the sealing jaws. On FOOD LINE VFFS frames this is paired with automatic film-deviation correction. Unstable film pull shows up as wrinkled seals, misplaced print or bags that vary in length.

Touch screen controller. The touch screen controller is where recipes, feeder amplitudes, target and tolerance, discharge timing and fault recovery live. Operators should be able to call up a product, see inhibited heads and understand why a cycle was rejected. FOOD LINE’s weigher-and-VFFS lines use PLC control, a touch screen and recipe memory, with “no product – no bag” and “no film – alarm” interlocks.

Packaging film roll. The packaging film roll should be chosen with the machine, not after it arrives. Width, thickness and sealant layer have to match the forming collar and jaw temperature. FOOD LINE’s weigher lines use roll-film widths up to 420, 620 or 820 mm on the FL-420W, FL-620W and FL-820W frames, with bag widths from 80 mm to 400 mm depending on the model. Confirm the actual film during a sample test, not only a dry cycle.

Together they turn a high-precision weighing filling machine into a reliable packer, but only when the dose, the film and the seal window stay in time.

Most buyers need a configured cell, not a bare scale. Shortlist from the setups below, then size the weigher and bagger against a sample of your product. The five-step packing machine buyer’s guide is useful when format and speed are still open.

An Automatic Snack Granule Weighing Sealing Machine is the typical snack cell: elevator, combination weigher and vertical bagger, often with nitrogen flushing. Applications: what changes on the factory floor

A snack food multi-head weighing packaging system is judged as much on breakage and bag appearance as on weight. Fried chips and puffed pieces are fragile and often oily. FOOD LINE’s published practice is a low-speed bucket elevator, adjustable vibration, gentle bucket opening and a short drop, with nitrogen flushing strongly recommended so the pack stays crisp. Details are on the potato chips and puffed snack and snack, nut and granule pages.

A granule product automatic weighing bagging unit is usually the cleaner engineering case. Free-flowing rice, beans, sugar or pellets fill hoppers predictably, so feeder tuning is simpler. The remaining risks are dust, film-seal contamination and hopper volume that does not match the target.

Nut quantitative weighing and sealing packaging equipment is where giveaway becomes a cost line, not just a quality metric. Piece weights vary, oil can affect discharge, and a gram over target on every bag adds up quickly. That is why FOOD LINE recommends a 14-head weigher for expensive products such as nuts and dried fruit, and why a giveaway test on the real product belongs in acceptance.

A hardware parts combination weighing packing machine has to settle a different argument: counting versus weighing. If piece weight is tight, a combination weigher can deliver a consistent pack weight that also corresponds to a count. If piece weight varies, “100 pieces” and “N grams” are not the same specification. State dust conditions and the required contact finish in the inquiry.

A frozen food precision weighing packaging production line has to cope with surface moisture, condensation and product that can clump or stick in cold rooms. FOOD LINE specifies dimpled or Teflon-coated buckets and an inclined or gentle-drop VFFS for sticky, fragile or frozen foods such as dumplings, with a cold-resistant design on its frozen food packing solutions page. Test both machines at packing temperature, not only on thawed samples.

How buyers should evaluate and source a line

In the industrial packaging equipment market, two quotations with the same head count can still describe different machines. One may be a fully integrated line with elevator, platform, bagger handshake and inspection. The other may be a standalone weigher that leaves feeding, timing and film performance for you to solve. Ask for the complete scope in writing.

The most reliable evidence is a run with your product inside a food processing factory workshop, or a witnessed factory acceptance test that records mean weight, spread, rejects, giveaway, accepted packs per minute, product damage, seal quality and changeover time. A clean demonstration on a free-flowing substitute will not show bridging, oil film or a long product tail into the seal area.

Live machines at an agricultural machinery exhibition hall are useful for comparing build quality, HMI layout, hopper removal and frame isolation. They are not a substitute for a timed run at your target weight. Use the show to eliminate poor access or fragile construction, then insist on a product test before you sign.

Factory-direct custom lines should also be weighed against off-the-shelf models you might see in a hardware and industrial supplies mall or a commercial packaging equipment store. Catalogue units can work when the product is simple and a standard hopper and film width will do. They are a poorer fit when you need coated contact parts, an inclined bagger, nitrogen flushing or a documented accuracy band on an irregular product. A line specified and tested as one system is then usually the lower-risk buy.

To size a FOOD LINE cell, send the product form, target weight, required speed, bag or jar format and electrical supply, and request a sample test on the contact page.

FAQ

What is the difference between a 10-head and a 14-head weigher?

A 14-head weigher has more hoppers and therefore many more possible weight combinations, which generally supports higher speed and tighter giveaway on irregular products. FOOD LINE advises that a 10-head weigher suits most snacks up to about 60 weighments/min, while a 14-head weigher is preferable for high-speed lines or costly products such as nuts and dried fruit. Decide on your product, target weight and accepted bag rate.

How accurate is a multi-head weigher packaging system?

On FOOD LINE’s published weigher-and-VFFS specifications, accuracy is ±1 g at or below 100 g and ±1 % above 100 g, depending on the product. Actual results move with piece size, stickiness, infeed stability, hopper volume and bagger synchronisation. Judge the line on bag-weight distribution and giveaway, not only on the average.

Can the same line pack snacks, granules, nuts and frozen food?

Often yes, if hoppers, coatings, drop height and recipes are specified for the most demanding product. A dry granule and a sticky frozen dumpling are not the same duty. Plan changeover parts and a recipe for each SKU, and test the difficult product rather than assuming a snack setup will transfer.

Do I need a check weigher after the bagger?

If you sell by weight in the EU, UK, USA or Gulf markets, FOOD LINE strongly recommends a check weigher to reject under-weight packs and document compliance. Even in other markets, it catches drift, inhibited heads, or a film or jamming event the weigher may not see.

How should the weigher be integrated with a VFFS machine?

Define the ready-to-discharge handshake, dose-complete signal, permitted hold time, and what happens on a fault or restart. Confirm that the last of the product tail clears the seal area before the jaws close. FOOD LINE supplies the weigher, platform and bagger as a tested line; if you pair machines from different sources, those signals still need to be written down and verified.

What information do you need to specify a line?

Send the product and its condition (size, oil, fragility, packing temperature), the target weight and tolerance, the required accepted packs per minute, the bag or container drawing, the film or pouch, and your voltage and available space. A sample test is the fastest way to turn those inputs into a firm recommendation.
